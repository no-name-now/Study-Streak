Embedded Hardware Selection
Before choosing a specific development board, engineers must determine whether their application requires a Microcontroller Unit (MCU) or a Single-Board Computer (SBC).
This is to prevent hardware misallocation in engineering projects, guiding engineers from selecting overly complex Linux boards for simple hardware control or underpowered microcontrollers for heavy computing tasks.

Arduino Uno
The Arduino Uno powered by the ATmega328P microcontroller is designed for low-complexity, real-time control applications.
Below is an overview of how these specific hardware parameters shape its practical usage:
1. CPU: ATmega328P (8-bit AVR RISC)
2. Clock Speed: 16 MHz
3. RAM: 2 KB (SRAM)
4. Flash Memory: 32 KB
Ideal Target Applications
Sensors & Actuators: Reading analog/digital sensors (temperature, ultrasonic, PIR) and controlling relays, servos, or LEDs.
Basic Automation: Small robotics (line-following or obstacle-avoiding rovers), home automation triggers, and switch controllers.

Arduino Uno Cont’d
Processing Power: Classic R3: Microchip ATmega328P (8-bit AVR RISC), 16 MHz clock speed, 2 KB SRAM, 32 KB Flash memory.
R4 Minima: Renesas RA4M1 (32-bit Arm Cortex-M4 with FPU) @ 48 MHz, 32 KB SRAM, 256 KB Flash.
Connectivity: Bare-board USB (Type-B or Type-C for programming/Serial communication). Lacks native Wi-Fi or Bluetooth; requires external peripheral expansion shields.
GPIO & Hardware Interfaces:
14 Digital I/O pins (6 support hardware PWM)
6 Analog Input pins (ADC).
Bus interfaces: 1× UART, 1× SPI, 1× I2C. 5V operating logic level (R3/R4).
Power Consumption: Very low baseline: ~45 mA (~0.22 W) at 5V DC in standard operating mode.
Basic sleep modes available down to microampere range for basic MCU sleep states.

Arduino Mega 2560
The Arduino Mega 2560 is an extended MCU platform designed for projects requiring expanded input/output capacity and multiple dedicated serial communication ports without switching away from the 8-bit Arduino environment.
Ease of Learning: High. Identical software workflow and API structures as the Arduino Uno. Ideal transition path for beginners encountering pin limitations.
Processing Power: Microchip ATmega2560 (8-bit AVR RISC), 16 MHz clock speed, 8 KB SRAM, 256 KB Flash memory, 4 KB EEPROM.
Connectivity: USB Type-B for serial/programming interface. No native wireless (Wi Fi/Bluetooth); relies on SPI/UART-attached network expansion shields.
GPIO & Hardware Interfaces:
54 Digital I/O pins (15 support PWM).
16 Analog Input pins (10-bit ADC).
Bus interfaces: 4× dedicated Hardware UART ports, 1× SPI, 1× I2C. 5V logic operation.
Power Consumption: Low baseline: ~70 mA (~0.35 W) at 5V DC. Higher static draw than the Uno due to the onboard USB interface chip and larger pin breakdown circuit.

Raspberry Pi 5
Unlike microcontrollers that run bare-metal code, the Raspberry Pi 5 is a fully realized Single Board Computer (SBC). It executes full operating systems (primarily Linux) and targets high-performance computing tasks.
Ease of Learning: Moderate to Advanced. Requires knowledge of Linux OS configuration, terminal navigation, system permissions, and high-level programming languages (Python, C++, JavaScript).
Processing Power: Broadcom BCM2712 Quad-core 64-bit Arm Cortex-A76 @ 2.4 GHz. 4 GB, 8 GB, or 16 GB LPDDR4X SDRAM. MicroSD / PCIe 2.0 (NVMe SSD) external storage interface.
Connectivity:
Integrated Dual-band (2.4 GHz / 5.0 GHz) 802.11ac Wi-Fi.
Bluetooth 5.0 & Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE).
Gigabit Ethernet (PoE+ supported via HAT).
2× USB 3.0, 2× USB 2.0, 2× 4K60 micro-HDMI outputs, PCIe Gen 2.0 interface.

GPIO & Hardware Interfaces:
40-pin GPIO Expansion Header (3.3V logic level).
Bus interfaces:
Software/Hardware configurable UART, SPI, I2C, I2S.

Power Consumption:
High (Requires active management).

Under full load: 8 W to 12 W+ (requires dedicated 5V/5A Power Supply and active fan cooling).

ESP32 [Espressif Systems]
The ESP32 is a low-power system-on-a-chip (SoC) microcontroller with integrated wireless capabilities. It is the dominant platform for Internet of Things (IoT) applications.
Ease of Learning: Moderate. Compatible with the Arduino IDE ecosystem (using C/C++ libraries), MicroPython, and ESP-IDF (C-framework). Requires attention to 3.3V logic levels and pin multiplexing quirks.
Processing Power:
Tensilica Xtensa Dual-Core 32-bit LX6 @ up to 240 MHz. 520 KB SRAM, supported by 4 MB to 16 MB external SPI Flash. Includes a Ultra-Low-Power (ULP) co-processor.
Connectivity:
On-chip Wi-Fi (802.11 b/g/n up to 150 Mbps).
On-chip Bluetooth v4.2 BR/EDR and BLE.

ESP32 [Espressif Systems] Cont’d
GPIO & Hardware Interfaces:
~34 Digital GPIO pins (3.3V logic level, not 5V tolerant).
18 channels of 12-bit ADC, 2 channels of 8-bit DAC.
Touch-capacitive GPIO pins.
Bus interfaces: 3× UART, 2× SPI, 2× I2C, 2× I2S (for digital audio), PWM channels.
Power Consumption:
Highly Dynamic / Ultra-Low Power Optimized.
Active TX mode (Wi-Fi transmitting): 160 mA – 260 mA (~0.8 W – 1.3 W).
Idle: ~20 mA – 50 mA.
Deep Sleep mode: ~10 µA – 15 µA (ideal for multi-year battery operation).
